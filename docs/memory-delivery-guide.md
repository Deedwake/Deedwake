# Memory and delivery: facilitator guide

This four-condition study supports a formative test of one fictional convoy
encounter. It makes retention and delivery independently selectable; it supplies
no human results or conclusion that either mechanism improves the experience.
Use the ordinary pages for participants. Do not explain the intended causal
chain or show the developer page before the trial.

## Preparation

Record the exact source/build artifact, device, participant experience, trial
order, instructions, assistance and deviations. Reading and choices take no
fictional time; only the explicit advance choice increments the clock. There is
no telemetry, upload or storage on these study pages. Record observations manually
with participant consent. Restart clears the fixture and retains its condition.
No save/resume is offered for these profiles.

| Study | Ordinary page | Retention | Initial / routine / intermediary delivery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A | `/memory-delivery-a.html` | Precise | Immediate / immediate / immediate |
| B | `/memory-delivery-b.html` | Incomplete, explicitly uncertain | Immediate / immediate / immediate |
| C | `/memory-delivery-c.html` | Precise | 3 / 3 / 1 fictional hours |
| D | `/memory-delivery-d.html` | Incomplete, explicitly uncertain | 3 / 3 / 1 fictional hours |

All four share the same witness opportunity: Mara sees the convoy held and a
visible loose-strap inspection. She does not observe the later release or gain
private motives. The precise account retains the visible inspection; the
incomplete account omits that context. The shared service rule uses the arrived
account and release evidence. Precise retention is not privileged truth, and an
incomplete account is not a new random memory roll on every question.

All four use the same actions, visual cues, layout and service rule. Text honestly
reflects the retained account and selected timing. The baseline is a complete
encounter with a useful service interaction; a restriction need not occur in
every condition. Study letters and client files are visible, so this is not a
claim of blinding. The older `/trial-a.html` and `/trial-b.html` compare only
correction-route timing; do not mix those trials with this four-condition study.

For a small group, use these counterbalanced orders, assigning rows without
selecting for an expected outcome: A–B–D–C, B–C–A–D, C–D–B–A, D–A–C–B. Record
which row was used. Four replays by one person involve learning and carryover;
they are not four independent participants or a statistical result.

## Participant task and questions

Ask the participant to check the loose strap, discover what Mara and Ivo know,
and decide how to handle any service problem. Let them choose when to ask,
release, contact Sela, advance and return. Keep the task wording the same across
conditions. Record actual action order and fictional hours, including ineffective
choices and assistance. Do not force a refusal or a repair that the condition
does not produce.

Before a return visit, ask what they expect Ivo knows and how they expect him to
respond. After each trial ask:

- What did Mara see? What did she remember or omit, and what remained unknown?
- What did Ivo receive, and when? How did you learn his current response?
- Why was service available, restricted or restored? Did that match your prediction?
- Did releasing the convoy change Ivo immediately, or was something still traveling?
- What did using Sela change? What did it leave unchanged?
- Which choices felt useful, confusing or like waiting? Did uncertainty seem like
  a character's limited account or a software error?

After all trials, ask which differences mattered and which could be simplified.
Record counterexamples, not only enjoyment. Encounter explanations supply
feedback; they do not independently prove the participant understood beforehand.

## Facilitator checks after play

For a hold at hour zero, an Ivo visit at hour three, then release at hour three:

| Study | Expected service history |
| --- | --- |
| A | Service stays available; the precise report arrives at hour 0. No restriction or restoration is invented. |
| B | Restricted at hour 0; restored at hour 3 when the release receipt arrives immediately. A witnessed earlier refusal remains history. |
| C | Available before the report and after its arrival at hour 3; a visit after arrival can name the observed inspection context. |
| D | Restricted at hour 3; routine release evidence restores at hour 6, or a handoff to Sela at hour 3 restores at hour 4. |

Immediate means receipt and policy evaluation in the dispatch action's current
hour, not at the next advance. Scheduled receipts never arrive early. The routine
correction remains queued when Sela sends another account. Correction may overtake
an initial report; late report arrival does not erase received correction or
reinstate a completed misunderstanding. Equal-time events use insertion order.
A remote arrival does not itself write the player's journal or change enabled
choices. Only conversations expose the resulting response. Earlier accounts,
refusals and confirmed consequences remain history.

For developer diagnosis only, `/memory-delivery-developer.html` provides a
compiled-condition selector and read-only actual Rust trace. Selecting a different
condition creates a fresh session. It exposes knowledge outside the player view
and must remain separate from human trials. The trace retains 128 entries and
shows the dropped-entry count; absence from an evicted trace is not proof that
an event never occurred. Encounter explanations use retained confirmed outcomes
and their evidence, independently of trace eviction. Time advancement stops at
hour 1000. Browser client inspection is not a secrecy boundary.

## Evidence still needed

This implementation makes the four comparisons runnable. It does not pass the
player-value gate, establish superiority, or replace the separate authoring
comparison with competent ordinary flags/scripts. Human observation, an outside
developer exercise, declared measured budgets and distribution decisions remain
separate work. If omission only confuses players or delay only adds waiting,
simplify those mechanisms rather than adding more simulation.
