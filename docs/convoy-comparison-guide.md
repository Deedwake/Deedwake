# Convoy delivery comparison: facilitator guide

This is an exploratory comparison of correction-delivery timing in one fictional
scene. It does not complete the broader evaluation or establish statistical
superiority. No human results are supplied by this guide.

## Conditions and preparation

- Trial A: `/trial-a.html`. Routine correction takes three fictional hours;
  Sela's verified pouch takes one.
- Trial B: `/trial-b.html`. Both corrections take three fictional hours.
- Use ordinary trial pages only. Developer traces are on `/developer.html`
  (current) and `/convoy-control-developer.html` (control).

Both trials use the same scene, observations, actions, policies and layout.
Timing text reflects each compiled condition. These pages do not promise blinded
conditions: participants can read their timing labels and inspect client files.
No telemetry is collected; record observations manually with participant consent.

For several participants, alternate A-then-B and B-then-A, keeping other
instructions and the test setting the same. Record the actual order. For one
person, treat the second trial as a familiar replay, not an independent sample.
Do not show the expected-results table below until both trials are finished.

## Trial procedure

1. Load the assigned ordinary page fresh. Confirm fictional time is zero and
   the journal is empty. Restart resets the session and retains that condition.
2. Hold the convoy, ask Mara, advance three fictional hours, and visit Ivo.
   Reading and questions do not advance time.
3. Release the convoy. Before advancing again, ask the participant what they
   think Ivo knows now and what they want to do next. Let them choose whether to
   give Sela the receipt immediately or rely on the routine correction.
4. Advance one fictional hour at a time and visit Ivo after each advancement
   until service returns. Record the route choice, release/send/return times,
   explanations and confusion. Record deviations rather than silently correcting
   them. A pouch sent later has a later due time.
5. Ask the questions below, then repeat on the other assigned page from a fresh
   session. Keep these instructions identical. Discuss differences afterward.

## Questions

- What did Mara actually see, and what was outside her view?
- What did Ivo know before and after you released the convoy? How did you learn
  whether his response changed?
- Why did you choose that delivery route? What did you expect it to change?
- When service returned, which evidence do you think permitted the change?
- After both trials: did the route's value change? What was confusing about the
  choices, timing labels, journal or earlier refusal?

## Facilitator check after the trials

For a hold at hour zero, refusal and release at hour three, and an immediate
Sela handoff when used, expected restoration is:

| Condition | Routine correction | Immediate Sela handoff |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / current | Hour 6 | Hour 4 |
| B / control | Hour 6 | Hour 6 |

The routine correction is queued on release in either route. The intermediary
adds another delivery; it does not cancel or rewrite the earlier account.
Remote arrival alone adds nothing to the player's journal. A visit provides the
new response, while earlier refusals remain historical entries.

Report concrete observations and uncertainty. Automated correctness checks and
positive participant feedback answer different questions; neither is a complete
usability or player-value evaluation.
